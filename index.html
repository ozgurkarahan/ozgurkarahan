Three questions I bring to enterprise AI.
The projects below explore them in different ways.

A task, not just an answer.

01 — What can it change?

Horizon Famille. A customer calls about a booking. The agent checks the situation, quotes a change before acting, and routes a discount outside its mandate to a person in Teams.

The useful part is the journey: a request becomes a concrete business action, with a visible point of human decision.

Watch the call 6m19s↗ Inspect the source ↗ Open the presentation ↗

Demonstration scope. A real recorded phone call through Voice Live and Azure Communication Services; mock CRM, ERP, and knowledge-base services; Power Platform approval in Teams. Not live Dynamics or SAP integrations, a production deployment, or a public number to call.

The caller's identity matters.

02 — Whose authority does it use?

An agent's connection to a business system should not erase the person making the request.

These separate implementations explore caller identity through Entra ID, Foundry, API Management, and downstream systems. Carrying an identity is only part of the job: authorization still needs to be enforced at each boundary.

  • Entra ID identity flow ↗Proof of concept
  • Salesforce meta-tool ↗Sample implementation
  • ServiceNow meta-tool ↗Sample implementation

These samples are not audited production guarantees. Read the evaluation boundaries before adapting a pattern.

A capable agent also needs a stop line.

03 — When should a person decide?

More autonomy is not automatically a better outcome. My starting point is to make the permitted action, the escalation condition, and the human decision explicit.

A useful handoff explains what is being requested, why approval is needed, and what the person can decide. Horizon's discount-approval workflow is one concrete example of that broader principle.
