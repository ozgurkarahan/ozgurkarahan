Enterprise AI architecture

Models matter. What surrounds them decides whether agents get used.

A better model arrives every few weeks, and that matters. But in real enterprise projects, an agent succeeds or fails on everything around it: people talking to it, finding it in Teams or Copilot, reaching the company's workflows and data, acting as the person who asked, and staying under rules everyone can see.

Those five topics matter more every month. They are what I build and test, in the open.

Not covered here, yet: cost control and the value of each token. They are coming fast too.

01 · The new channel

Voice

Voice is becoming the way we talk to agents.

Typing was the first interface. Speaking is faster and more natural, and it is spreading quickly: customers call voice agents, and even developers now talk to their coding agents. A voice agent has to listen, act, and know when to hand over to a person.

03 · Workflows and data

Context

An agent is only as good as the company knowledge it can reach.

Bring the company's knowledge to the agent in three ways: MCP servers for workflows and data in systems such as Salesforce, ServiceNow or SAP; knowledge bases; and retrieval (RAG) over company documents. Each choice trades freshness, cost and control differently.

04 · From the voice to the system

Identity

The agent acts as the person who asked.

Identity is not a layer; it runs through all of them. Agents take more and more actions for users, in more and more systems, so the user's identity has to travel from the voice that asks, through Teams or Copilot, all the way to Salesforce or ServiceNow. Each system then applies the authentication and authorization rules it already has, instead of rebuilding them in middleware or hiding everyone behind one service account.

05 · Across every layer

Governance

Every agent, tool and skill needs an owner and a rule.

Governance is not a layer either; it applies at every one. As agents multiply, so do their tools and skills: which channels an agent is published to, which workflows and data it may touch, which skills it carries, where it runs, and where a person must decide. Agents that code or browse also need their own isolated compute, a sandbox per session rather than a shared server.
